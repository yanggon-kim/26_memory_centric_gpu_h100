## 1. Port bug: the second dispatch lane collided with the first

- **What happened:** the `--se-study` plan runs the dispatch suite on lanes L0 and L1 with the same cell names.
  Attempt directories are per cell, so L1's first dispatch step found L0's directory and exited with
  `FileExistsError`. That ended L1's pilot lane; its 11 placement steps had already run. Its production steps
  (E2, `e6_balanced`, `e6_decode` blocks) ran normally.
- **Effect on the tables:** the dispatch table in `summary.md` lists every row twice. Both copies are L0's data,
  because the analyzer maps both plan entries to the one directory. `hse_judgment.json` reads the raw files and
  uses L0 only.
- **Effect on the verdict:** none. P1m needed the full-device prediction to hold on both GPUs, and it already
  fails on L0, in agreement with the smoke on a third GPU (499333).
- **Fix (v1.6.1, `plan.py`):** `dispatch_suite` takes a `tag`, and the 8-GPU SE study tags its cells `_L0` and
  `_L1`. The regenerated plan has 371 unique cells. The 497082 follow-up plan regenerates unchanged, and
  `test_analyze.py` has 0 failures. Nothing was rerun: the 2-attempt rule is not needed for a verdict that is
  already decided.

### Verdict (pre-registered rule): **H_SE is refuted on MI300X**

P1m fails, so the rule says refuted. The specific claim that failed is that the **full** MI300X device is hurt
by its 9,10,10,9 layout. It is not: the gfx942 dispatcher follows that layout, the stock kernel does not
serialize at 304, and sizing for 288 gains at most 0.2%.

## 3. What the job shows beyond the verdict (not pre-registered as a whole; observational)

**At reduced CU counts, the stock decode penalty on MI300X is a dispatch artefact, and sizing removes it.**
- Under any mask below 304, stock `wvSplitK` still launches 304 workgroups. The dispatch probe shows 16–176 of
  them serialized behind another workgroup on the same CU (right-hand column above).
- Kernel traces (`e6_kernels_se.json`; `wvSplitK` is 83% of decode kernel time): its mean time relative to stock
  304 is **1.090 at 272 stock**, against 0.981–0.990 when sized to balanced(n) at 272, 232, 200 and 152.
  Trace timings carry the trace distortion; the ratios share it.
- **With the kernel sized to a balanced count, decode stays at full-device speed at every level tested, down to
  128 of 304 CUs (42%).** All 9 sized cells sit at R 0.993–0.998.
- **Earlier result revised:** 492956 found "the resized kernel is slower at every level" on MI300X (272 1.043 …
  152 1.202). Every level it resized to (272, 248, 232, 216, 200, 184, 152) is SE-unbalanced. That was the same
  dispatch artefact, not a bandwidth limit, as on MI350X in 497082.

**The B_CU picture across the two cohorts, with the artefact removed** (balanced-size kernel; peak HBM / CUs):

| cohort | CUs doing the work | B_CU needed | R |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MI300X | 288 / 256 / 224 / 192 | 18.4 / 20.7 / 23.7 / 27.6 GB/s | 0.996–0.998 |
| MI300X | 128 | 41.4 GB/s | 0.993 |
| MI350X (497082) | 192 | 41.7 GB/s | 1.009 |
| MI350X (497082) | 160 | 50.0 GB/s | 1.067 |
| MI350X (497082) | 128 | 62.5 GB/s | 1.168 |

- Both cohorts run decode at full speed up to about **42 GB/s of HBM bandwidth per CU**, and MI350X shows the
  cost rising above that. The two cohorts meet at the same B_CU (MI300X 128 CUs and MI350X 192 CUs).
- R slightly below 1 on MI300X is probably clock: fewer active CUs run at higher gfx clocks (pooled ratio up to
  1.49 at 128). This is why none of it is a non-inferiority claim.
- **Practical rule, now on both cohorts:** size `wvSplitK` to the largest SE-balanced CU count ≤ the active CUs
  (a multiple of 32 on MI300X and MI350X). On MI300X the full device needs no change.
