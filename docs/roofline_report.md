# H100 roofline control and analytical decode proxies — 2026-10-13

The exclusive H100 BF16 `8192×8192` GEMM control measured a median **718.40 TFLOP/s** over three GPU-event trials after three warmups. A separate 1 GiB device-to-device copy buffer, larger than twice the queried 50 MiB L2, measured **3032.09 useful GB/s** (source reads plus destination writes) over three trials. These are observed operation and useful-byte ceilings for the chosen CUDA 12.8 torch controls, not theoretical H100 peaks or direct HBM counters. Raw trial timings and device metadata are in `results/roofline_ceilings_cu128.json`.

`results/roofline_proxy_bridge_cu128.json` combines those controls with existing BF16 Qwen2.5-1.5B bridge decode wall timings. Its model counts full non-embedding dense matrices once per batch step, estimated attention FLOPs, and BF16 KV reads at the average attended length. The ratios are analytical read proxies. For B1/C1024 compiled, B1/C128 eager, and B8/C4096 eager, the respective proxy intensities are approximately **1.06, 1.01, and 7.47 FLOP/read-byte**. Their timing-derived proxy throughputs are approximately **0.98, 0.18, and 1.70 TFLOP/s**. The B8 compiled failure is excluded by its strict correctness gate; the eager B8 control is a distinct program.

The control envelope uses the measured GEMM and useful-copy rates only as a comparison. Neither actual decode FLOPs nor HBM/L2 traffic were measured. Launch overhead, cache residency, weight reuse, KV page layout, and scheduler behavior can move real points materially. This plot/table cannot distinguish HBM saturation from memory latency, cache/NoC/issue pressure, or other limits. Recompute the proxies for optimized vLLM only after its correctness and prefill gates pass; hardware-counter roofline points remain unavailable while Nsight Compute reports `ERR_NVGPUCTRPERM`.
